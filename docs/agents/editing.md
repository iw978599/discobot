# Editing notes, steps, automation, scenes and songs

Part of `AGENTS.md`, moved here to keep that file small. Read it before changing the starting-points strip, Copy bar and Copy to, the song's views, scene colours, chords and note lengths, Transpose, Randomize, automation and Auto, or a step's chance, repeats, offset, strum and slide, and keep it up to date as you would `AGENTS.md`.

- The starting-points strip is the first-run choice (roadmap V15): three plain answers, Hear a song, Start from a beat, and Start empty (which puts the strip away). The songs are `SAMPLE_SONGS` in `sampleSongs.ts`, seven in different styles and time signatures (one, Lanterns Over Dark Water, a seven-minute post-rock piece on all five named lanes whose loud and quiet are made by automating levels, filters, drive and reverb sends, asked for by the owner on 2026-10-19), each written as data (`SampleSong` in `sampleSong.ts`: lanes by built-in preset id with a sound laid over it, scenes with their signature, bars, notes as `Placed`, drums as `Hits` and automation, the kit, drum settings such as a percussion or another cymbal, and effects laid over `EFFECTS_DEFAULT`) and built by `buildSampleSong` as a project file from the open project's file, then opened through `handleImportProject`, so each is a new project and nothing is overwritten or needs undoing. A song names its sounds by preset id: renaming or removing one breaks it, and `sampleSong.test.ts` opens every song through the store and fails if anything had to be repaired. An aux bus's `sync` takes only `RATE_SYNCS` (no dotted values), unlike the shared delay's. The owner asked on 2026-10-20 that no lane in a song says Generic and that every lane is named: each lane is a synth model's preset, or a recorded instrument, which a lane calls by its preset's name. A song sets its overall level (`level`, which scales every lane's level and every automated level, so a swell keeps its shape) and its drums' (`drumVolume`). The owner heard clipping in the post-rock song, whose Storm summed to full scale before any effect: each song's levels were then set so that its loudest section's dry mix (lanes and drums, rendered with `renderSynthBars` and `renderDrums`) peaks near 0.5, leaving room for the effects; keep to that when a song changes. The first song, Night Circuit (dark synthwave in F minor), replaced Neon Drive at the owner's request. The songs were written by reasoning and have not been heard; the owner asked for them on 2026-10-19 to show what the app can do. A starting beat still changes no sound; it sets each hit's strength (`velocities`) as well as where the hits are, and a few hits' chance and repeats (`chances`, `repeats`). `starterDrums` writes a drum's chances and repeats whenever the grid already holds some, so those of a beat tried before do not stay under the next; keep that if a beat is given anything else per step.
- A project that is opened with one scene, no note and no drum hit shows the starting-points strip above the song unit (`showStarters`, decided each time a project is put on the rack). Choosing one sets the tempo, the drum grid and Synth 1's pattern as one undo step and changes no sound. The strip leaves only by its own Start empty button: never take it, or any other unit, away because of an edit made below it, which would move the rack under the pointer that made the edit. Most browser tests start on an empty project, so the strip is on screen in them.
- Copy bar (`CopyBar`, shown beside the bar buttons of a lane or the drum grid that is longer than one bar) lays the bar on show over another bar of the same pattern, or over every other. It is an edit to the open scene, so it is one undo step and does not ask. A lane's goes through `writePattern` (`handleCopyLaneBar`); the drums' is the `drum.copyBar` operation, which takes every drum's steps with their velocities, chances and repeats. `barTargets` and `copyBar` in `patternLength.ts` hold the rule for both. Its wrapper carries the class `copy-to` as well, for the menu's placing: a test that looks for Copy to by that class must also ask for the button's name.
- Copy to (`CopyToScene`, the `scene.copyPart` operation) puts one lane's notes or one drum's row from the open scene into another scene's stored copy. Undo takes it back like any other edit; the control still asks before replacing something, because what it replaces is out of sight. A lane brings its own length; a drum row is laid along the other scene's grid, repeating or stopping short, and brings its velocities, chances and repeats with it.
- The song's order can be shown two ways, chosen in the song unit and kept per browser (`songView.ts`, like `rackView.ts`): the blocks, which are the default, and the playlist (`SongPlaylist.tsx`), a row for each entry with its bars, when it starts and how long it lasts. Both edit the same `song.entries` through `handleSongChange` and neither holds anything of its own; the playlist's rows come from `songRows` and its edits from `moveSongEntry`, `removeSongEntry`, `insertSongEntry` and `repeatSongEntry` in `songPlayback.ts`, which also say where the marked starting entry is afterwards. An edit arrives a moment after its button is pressed, so the playlist moves keyboard focus to the row that was edited once the new entries are in (`focusNext`). The playlist wraps its rows and has no rules in the phone block. Browser tests other than `song-playlist.spec.ts` are written for the blocks.
- The song's third view is the timeline (`TrackTimeline`, stage 3 of `docs/TIMELINE_PLAN.md`): the song's entries laid along their ticks (2px a tick, so a 4/4 bar is 64px) with the audio tracks under them, each row's head sticky while the bars scroll inside `.timeline-scroll`, never the page. Where a clip is and how wide comes from `songSecondsAt` and its inverse `songTickAt`, so it follows each scene's tempo. A drag moves a clip by beats (Alt: steps), to another track by the lane under the pointer (`data-track-lane`), or trims it by an end (`.timeline-trim`, on the chosen clip only, with no `onClick`); it is drawn as a preview and written once, when let go. With the keyboard a clip (a `role="button"` that is a Tab stop) moves by the arrows, to another track by up and down (the keyboard follows it, `refocus`), and is taken out by Delete. Add Recording keeps the file as a sample (`handleAddRecording`), decodes it for its length, and puts it whole at the bar mark on the chosen track, making a track when there is none, in one undo step. The mark, the chosen track and the chosen clip are view state.
- Scenes, the song's blocks and the playlist's rows can be dragged to another place (`useDragOrder` in `ui/src/rack/useDragOrder.ts`, roadmap 20). A mouse or pen picks an item up once it moves past `TAP_SLOP`; a finger must rest `TAP_HOLD_MS` first, so a finger still scrolls; where it is let go is the item under the pointer with the same `data-order-list`; the click that ends a drag is swallowed, and Escape puts it back. A scene moves with the `scene.move` edit (its place in `scenes` only; the song and the open scene are untouched), and also with Alt and an arrow key on its chip, which keeps the focus on it. Blocks and rows move with `moveSongEntry`, so the marked start entry follows. Lanes do not move: the owner has not said whether that renumbers them. A spec drags with `page.mouse`, moving a little first to pick the item up. Not tried on a real phone.
- A scene can carry a colour (`scene.color`, one of the names in `sceneColors.ts`, absent for none), drawn as a band on its chip, on its blocks in the song and on the share page. `commitScene` rebuilds the open scene from the live lanes, so it hands the colour back to `captureScene`; anything else added to a scene that is not steps or mutes must be carried the same way, or the first edit loses it. A scene made with + Copy or + Empty takes the first colour no scene has; the scene a project starts with has none.
- The piano roll's Chord menu (`services/chords.ts`) is how the roll is being used, not part of the song: with a shape chosen, a note put in is handed to `handlePianoRollNoteAssign` as the whole chord, which is one write and one undo entry. Notes the roll has no row for are left out. Taking a note out is always one note. The Inversion beside it (`chordNotes`'s third argument) keeps the clicked note where it is and takes the notes above it down an octave, rather than the root up, so the clicked cell always holds a note; in the roll's lowest octave the notes taken down have no row and are left out. It is view state of `PianoRoll`, like the shape.
- Each note of a chord can have a length of its own (`step.lengths`, lowest note first), stored only where the notes differ; `step.length` is then the longest of them, so whatever only knows `length` (the step row, the arpeggiator's window, repeats, an older version of the app) still has the step's whole extent. Read a note's length with `noteLengths(step)` and set it with `withStepLength(step, length, note?)`; never write `lengths` by hand, and anything that scales or cuts `length` must do the same to `lengths` (`clampLengths` does). In the piano roll, dragging a note's end changes that note alone unless Whole step is on; the step controls' Note length is always the whole step.
- A step can hold a chord and last several steps. `note` is the lowest note and `notes` the rest (up to six in all); `length` is in steps and absent means one. Never read `step.note` to find what a step plays: use `stepNotes(step)`, and change a chord with `withStepNotes` so the order and the limit hold. With the arpeggiator on, a chord is arpeggiated through its own notes.
- Transpose (`TransposeTool`, in a lane's Notes tab) rewrites the notes of that lane's pattern in the open scene, by a semitone or an octave; it is not a playback setting, and the drums have none. The arithmetic is `transposeSteps` in `services/transpose.ts`, and `handleTransposeLane` writes it as one undo step. If any note would cross out of C0 to B8 nothing moves and the button is disabled, so a move can always be undone by the opposite one; never move some notes and not others. `withStepNotes` keeps a note's own length by its name, and after a move a name can belong to another note of the chord, so the lengths are put back by position. Built as a draft: what is moved and whether it should be a setting are the owner's to decide (roadmap, features 19).
- The scale lock (roadmap 16, `services/scaleLock.ts`): Randomize's key and scale are one store for the whole studio (`scaleLock`, read with `useSyncExternalStore`), with a Lock switch beside them in every lane's Randomize row (`Scale lock`), and all of it is this browser's (`discobot_randomize_v1`, `discobot_scale_lock_v1`), never the project's. With the lock on: a key played by hand sounds as the nearest note of the scale, the lower of two as near (`toScale`), in `useStudioNotes` (`handleNotePlay` remembers what each key became until it is let go; a MIDI note is moved where it is named, so `midiHeldRef` holds the moved name); the keyboard and the piano roll mark notes outside it (`out-of-scale`), and the roll puts no new note there, though one already there can be taken out; and any chord shape keeps only its notes in the scale. The chord menu's Triad, Seventh and Ninth in key (`KEY_CHORD_SHAPES`, `keyChord`) are every other note of the scale from the root, and are offered with the lock off too. Notes already in a pattern are never changed by it. The owner confirmed all of this on 2026-10-20 and asked for more scales and chords: `RANDOM_SCALES` has sixteen, kept by id (never rename one, since the choice is stored by it), and `CHORD_SHAPES` are in `CHORD_GROUPS`, shown as the Chord menu's groups; a new shape needs a group.
- Randomize (`RandomizeTool`, in a lane's Notes tab) replaces the notes of that lane's pattern in the open scene with a new line in a chosen key and scale, as one undo step, and touches nothing else. `randomLine` in `services/randomize.ts` makes it and takes its random source as an argument: the app passes `Math.random`, a test a seeded one. The line is one note to a step from `randomPool`, which starts at the key's root in the lowest octave the lane's piano roll shows and has nothing below it (a lower note would be off the roll); a lane with finer steps gets the same rhythm on every second step. The key and scale are kept per browser (`discobot_randomize_v1`), never in the project. The drums' Fill button is separate and unchanged. It does not ask before replacing a lane's notes. The owner agreed to all of this on 2026-10-13 (roadmap, features 20); what it makes is not verified by ear.
- A lane's automation is `pattern.automation` (open scene) and `scene.laneAutomation` (stored scenes): for each automated parameter, by its `LANE_PARAMS` key, a value per step or null. `captureScene`/`applyScene` carry it like `laneBars`, `sceneBar` slices it, and `sanitizeAutomation` keeps only `AUTOMATABLE` keys, at most `MAX_AUTOMATED`, clamped and as long as the steps; none is stored as no key at all, so an old project normalizes as it did. The store keeps a pattern's automation when a write sends none, so a write that means "none" sends `sentAutomation(undefined)` (`{}`): Clear, MIDI import, a starter, Reset. Anything that moves, copies, cuts or resizes a lane's steps must do the same to its automation with the matching function in `automation.ts`; Transpose and Randomize leave it alone.
- Automation only plays: it never changes the lane's sound. At each step the scheduler hands `stepVoiceParams` (every automated parameter, the step's value or the sound's own) to `synthAudio.automate`, timed to the step's audio-clock time; the core holds a timed `setParams` until its sample (`scheduled` in `SynthCore`, which cuts the block there and is one plain `render` call when nothing waits). While a lane is automated, `useSynthAudio` leaves those parameters out of every untimed update, or a knob turn or a played note would undo the step's value early. `automationChange` puts back a parameter the step before set and this one does not. `renderSynthBars` sends the same values at the same times, so export and live agree; `automation.test.ts` holds the two to one sequence. A lane's sends (`SEND_PARAMS`: reverb, delay, chorus, aux A and B) are automatable too, on a path of their own, because they are mixer levels outside the voice: `stepSends` gives a step's whole set of send levels (the step's value or the sound's own), the scheduler hands it to `synthAudio.automate` with the voice's, and the lane's audio reaches it at the step's time (`setSends` with a time, gliding by `SEND_GLIDE`). While a lane's sends are automated (`automatedSends` in `useSynthAudio`) a change to the sound leaves them alone, and Stop puts the sound's back. An export builds `sendTimeline` for each lane and `connectEffects` schedules each send along it, building an effect for any send that reaches above nothing at some step (and `effectsTail` counts an aux bus only automation sends to); with no send automated the graph is the one it always was. The duck is still not automatable.
- Auto (`autoRecording`, a session setting like Rec) records a knob only while the lane plays and not in a count-in: `handleParameterChange` asks `recordAutomation`, which writes the moved automatable values at the heard step (`recordedStep(id, 0)`), fills the steps passed since that parameter's last write with its last value, and leaves the sound alone; the scheduler plays the held value meanwhile. A pass ends at Stop (written up to the heard step), Auto off, or a scene change, and is one undo step, held open with `holdUndo('auto')` from its first write until the passes end. A preset load goes through `changeSound`, never through recording.
- A macro turned while Auto records, on screen or by a controller's knob tied to it (`paramTargets.ts` sends a tied macro through the same `handleSynthModelChange`), is recorded as the parameters it sets (`mapSynthModelToEngineParams`, through `recordAutomation`), since a macro is several parameters and not automated itself; the macro and the sound are left alone, as for any knob. Asked for by the owner on 2026-10-20 ("MIDI controls should be captured in parameter automation when used"). A tie to a drum or an effect still records nothing: only lanes have automation (roadmap 28).
- A step also has optional `probability` (chance), `ratchet` (repeats across the step's length) and `offset` (how late it starts, as a fraction of a step, which is how a note sits between steps). A chord can be strummed (`strum`, the time between one note and the next as a fraction of a step, up to `MAX_STRUM`, a half): `expandStepNotes` starts each note that much after the one below it and shortens it by the same, so they end together, and MIDI export does the same in ticks; the arpeggiator ignores it. `withStepNotes` drops all of them when a step is emptied. `expandStepNotes` takes a `random` argument: pass `seededRandom` in exports.
- A note's length is dragged by the handle on its last piano-roll cell. The handle moves as the note grows, so the drag is followed by listeners on `window`, not on the handle.
- The piano roll paints while a pressed pointer moves. It ignores cells that arrive under a pointer that has not moved (selecting a step can shift the layout), or one click would add a second note.
- A step's `slide` flag holds its note into the next step; a mono lane then glides instead of retriggering. Accent is step velocity routed to the filter (`velocity.filter`).
- Spread (`euclid` in `services/euclid.ts`, the menu beside the drums' Fill, asked for 2026-10-22) puts so many hits along the selected drum's bar on show as evenly as the bar's steps allow, the first on its first step, in place of what was on that bar; the hits keep their strengths. It writes through `onStepToggle`, so it is one undo step. The counts offered are two to one short of the bar's steps. Shift turns the result. A synth lane has no Spread.
