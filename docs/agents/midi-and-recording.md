# MIDI, recording and the metronome

Part of `AGENTS.md`, moved here to keep that file small. Read it before changing MIDI in or out, pads, Learn, devices, pitch bend and the wheel, recording notes, or the click and count-in, and keep it up to date as you would `AGENTS.md`.

- Pitch bend (`SynthCore.setBend`, -1 to 1, `BEND_SEMITONES` = 2 each way) bends every voice of the lane the device's notes go to (`liveLane` in `handleMidiMessage`: the default place needs the keyboard's channel; Drums and Off ignore it). It is live only: nothing goes through `localService`. The bend is let go when the transport stops (`stopAllNotes`), the device goes (`deviceGone` from `useMidiInput`) or its place changes; the wheel is let go on the last two but stays put on a stop. With no bend and no wheel routed, the core is bit-identical to before. Not verified by ear or with real hardware.
- Incoming notes on the drum channel (`midiDrumChannel`, 10 unless changed, 0 for none; a session setting like the rest of MIDI) are pads: `drumForNote` in `services/midiDrums.ts` gives each General MIDI drum note a lane, the eight a MIDI export writes going back where they came from. A pad plays its drum, and in record mode while the transport runs it is written on the step being played, with its velocity. If the keyboard's channel is the same one, the lane keeps the notes. Not tried against real hardware.
- A note played while the transport runs is recorded by `recordNote` in `useStudio`, for MIDI in record mode and, with Rec on in a lane's keyboard panel, for the on-screen and computer keyboards. It is written on the step the player is hearing, not the one the scheduler has run ahead to: `recordPosition` in `services/recordTiming.ts` works back from `BrowserTransport.ticksAt` on the audio clock (less the output latency), then moves the note towards the nearest step by the quantize strength, keeping what is left as the step's `offset`. Never write a recorded note at `playhead.get(lane)`: that is up to a step early. A recorded note lasts as long as its key was held (`recordHeldRef` in `useStudio`): the key's start on the transport clock is kept, and `handleNoteRelease`, which every way of letting a note go reaches, rounds the time held to steps (at least one, never past the end of the pattern) and gives that note of the chord that length with `withStepLength`. While any recorded key of a lane is down, its edits are one undo step (`holdUndo('record:<lane>')`), so a note and its length undo together; the transport stopping closes the group and keeps what was held. Nothing is recorded during a count-in. Rec and Quantize are session settings.
- A parameter anywhere in the project has a name (`services/paramTargets.ts`): `lane:1:cutoff`, `lane:open:cutoff` for whichever lane is open, `drum:kick:volume`, `drums:master`, `fx:delay.feedback`, `tempo`. Each has a range, and `fromUnit` turns a knob's travel (0 to 1) into a value in it. `applyParamTargetRef` in `useStudio` sets a parameter by name through the very handlers the knobs on screen call, so undo, saving and the sound treat the two alike. Anything else that needs to say "this parameter" (automation, the modulation routes one day) uses these names; do not invent a second way. Add a parameter by adding it to the table there; a test holds every lane parameter's range inside what a stored sound allows.
- A controller's knob is tied to a parameter by Learn in the MIDI panel (`services/midiTies.ts`): choose the parameter, press Learn, turn the knob. Ties are this browser's (`discobot_midi_ties_v1`), not the project's; a knob has one parameter and a parameter one knob. The value jumps to where the hardware knob is. Not tried against real hardware.
- Each drum's pad is one note and each note one drum (`PadMap` in `services/midiDrums.ts`, kept in this browser as `discobot_midi_pads_v1` and read through `asPadMap`). Learn beside a drum in the MIDI panel gives it the next note played (`withPad`), and the drum that had the note is left with none; Reset pads is `defaultPads`, the notes MIDI export writes. `drumForNote(note, pads)` also lets a General MIDI note near a drum play it, but only while that drum is still on its General MIDI note, so an untouched map is what it was before pads could be changed and a drum given a note by hand answers to that note alone. MIDI export never reads the map. Not tried against real hardware.
- A row added after the eight drums (`docs/DRUM_ROWS_PLAN.md`) has no pad until Learn gives it one (`PadMap` holds an added row's note only once learnt, and keeps it for every project; the MIDI panel lists this project's added rows, `drumRows`). It is written to a MIDI file, played out through a MIDI output and sent from the sequencer on `drumRowNote` (`utils/midiExport.ts`): its recording's own General MIDI note (a percussion or a cymbal), or a note of its own (64 to 71) that none of the eight uses. The eight are written in their order as before, then the added rows, so a file without them is byte for byte what it was.
- One Learn waits at a time, a knob's (`midiLearn`) or a pad's (`midiPadLearn`): `handleMidiLearn` and `handleMidiPadLearn` each call the other off, and the panel disables every other Learn while one waits. Nothing else may set either.
- Every listened MIDI input has a place its notes go (`services/midiDevices.ts`, kept in this browser as `discobot_midi_devices_v1`, by the device's name because a browser's device ids are its own). `default` ("As set above") is what there was before: the keyboard channel to the lane chosen in the panel, the Pads channel to the drums. `open`, `synth:1` to `synth:3` and `drums` take every note from the device whatever its channel, and `off` has the device ignored, knobs included. Only a place other than `default` is stored. The panel lists the devices only when there are two or more, or one that has a place of its own. `handleMidiMessage` is handed the input with each message and decides from that; recording writes to the lane the device is sent to.
- A lane holds a MIDI note under its device, channel and note (`midiHeldRef`), and a note-off is looked up there before anything else, so it reaches the lane that took the note-on whatever has changed since. `handleMidiDestinationChange` lets go of what a device's lanes hold before it moves the device; an unplugged device's notes are let go by `useMidiInput`. Keep both, or a key held across either is stuck.
- MIDI output is sent from the scheduler (`triggerStep`, the drum loop and the top of `scheduleTick`) with time stamps from `midiTime`, which turns an audio-clock time into Web MIDI's clock. Synth lane N is channel N and the drums channel 10, with the notes MIDI export uses, unless another channel is chosen for a part in the MIDI panel (`MidiOutChannels`, read with `outChannel` and `midiOut.channelOf`); clock is three pulses per transport tick with start and stop. The chosen output and what it is sent are session settings, not saved with the project. Notes played by hand (the on-screen and computer keyboards, a controller's keys and pads) are sent at once when Played is on (`midiOut.played`, from the one place that also hands them to a live session, `played` in `useStudioNotes`); a note still down when Played goes off is let go, and a release goes out on the channel its note went on. A controller that listens to its own output may then sound twice; the panel says so.
- Following another device's clock (Follow clock in the MIDI panel, `midiFollowClock`, a session setting): the parser reads the one-byte real-time messages (`clock`, `start`, `continue`, `stop`, each with its arrival time) and the input hook hands a pulse on without setting any state. Start and Continue play from the start and Stop stops (`handleGlobalPlayStopRef`, so it is this browser's alone in a live session), with no count-in. The tempo is `createClockFollower`'s reading of the last beat's 24 pulses, to a tenth, held in `externalTempoRef`/`externalTempo` and never written to the project; `tempoAt` in the transport answers it before any scene's or the project's, and the tempo display shows it as CLOCK and cannot be typed in. Turning Follow clock off gives the project's tempo back. It follows the clock's rate, not each beat's place, so a long play may drift a little from the other device. A device set to Off is not followed. Audio cannot start from a clock until the page has been clicked once.
- Aftertouch (channel pressure, and a key's own, taken as the lane's) is the route source Pressure (`pressure`, the ninth of `MOD_SOURCES`, kept in the same place in `SynthCore`'s list), set live through `setLive` like the wheel and let go with it. Not saved or exported.
- A MIDI file is imported at the smallest of 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars that holds it (`barsFor`), the same length for every track; anything past eight bars is left out and the dialog says so.
- The metronome (`services/metronome.ts`, Click and Count on the transport) is this browser's setting, not the project's, and never in an export. `scheduleClick` in `browserAudio.ts` plays a click into the master level, past the lanes and effects. The count-in is in the scheduler, not the transport: `scheduleTick` clicks its bars and returns, and takes them off the bar number for everything after, so the song, the lanes and MIDI start count from where the music starts. Guests are told the first beat is after the count-in. An export that plays the arrangement through to record guests has no count-in and no click (`exportingRef`). The count-in lasts 1, 2 or 4 bars (`bars`, `COUNT_IN_CHOICES`) and the click has a level (`level`, 0 to 1, where `CLICK_LEVEL` is the level it always had and `scheduleClick`'s `loudness` is the level over it); `asMetronome` keeps either only where it differs from before, so settings stored earlier read the same. Read them with `countInBars` and `clickLevel`, never the fields.
