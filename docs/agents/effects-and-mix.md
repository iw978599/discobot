# Effects, sends and the audio graph

Part of `AGENTS.md`, moved here to keep that file small. Read it before changing inserts, the shared effects, the aux buses, effect presets, the Hall, the audio engine, drum sends or ducking, and keep it up to date as you would `AGENTS.md`.

- A lane has insert effects of its own (`SynthParameters.inserts`: drive, tone, chorus, delay), run by `InsertChain` in `engine/src/synth/inserts.ts` at the end of `SynthCore.render`, so live playback and export are the same code and nothing about them is in the worklet wrapper or `wavExport.ts`. They come after the voices, the lane's level and pan, and before the lane reaches the mixer and its sends (the lane's level is therefore before the drive, so it changes how hard the drive works). `inserts` is absent from `createDefaultSynthParameters()` on purpose: absent means none, `sanitizeInserts` stores an effect only where it differs from `INSERT_DEFAULT` (`effectSettings.ts`), and a lane with none stores no key, so older projects load, hash and sound as before (and `SCHEMA` was not bumped). The core flattens them as `insDrive` to `insDelayFeedback` (`flatInserts` in `voiceParams.ts`), with a tempo-synced chorus rate and delay time worked out there from the tempo. The Inserts page sends the whole `inserts` object on every change, with `sync: 'off'` for none, because the store's merge keeps what is left out. A whole sound with no `inserts` (a preset, Reset, undo) clears them: `synth.params` treats a patch carrying `WHOLE_SOUND` and no `inserts` as none. The effects clear their buffers when all are off, so no echo comes back later. Not verified by ear.
- There are two aux buses (`EffectsLoopState.aux`, a list of two `AuxBus`: A then B), each pointed at an echo, the Hall or a chorus (`AuxKind`), with its own return `level`, a `main` and a `shape` setting whose meaning depends on the kind (`AUX_LIMITS` in `auxSettings.ts`: time and feedback, decay and damping, rate and depth) and a `sync` for the echo's time and the chorus's rate. The owner's word on 2026-10-13 was two buses and the effects the builder's choice. Every lane (`fxSends.auxA`, `auxB`), the drum kit (`drumFx.sends`) and each guest (`guest.sends`) has a send to each. All of it is optional and absent means as before: a send is stored only above nothing (`sanitizeSends`, `guestSends`), `aux` is stored only when a bus differs from the starting pair (`sanitizeAux`: an echo, then a Hall), and none is in the defaults passed to `localService`. A control that can bring a send back to nothing always sends it (`normalizeFxSends`), or the store's merge keeps the old value. `createAuxEffect` in `browserAudio.ts` builds the effect for live playback and for `connectEffects` in `wavExport.ts`; live, a bus's effect is built the first time a lane sends to it (`syncAux`) and swapped, with the old one let go, when its kind changes; in an export only a bus that is sent to and has a level is built. The buses come back through the group's return like the five shared effects, and `effectsTail` lengthens an export's tail only for a bus that is sent to. The mixer has a strip for each bus's return (`level`), gathered by `mixerChannels` as `aux` and changed through the effects handler like the bus's Level knob, which the owner asked for on 2026-10-19 (roadmap plan item 7). Each lane's two aux sends (`auxASend`, `auxBSend`) and each bus's level (`fx:auxA.level`, `fx:auxB.level`, applied to the list of buses as a whole) can be learned (`paramTargets.ts`); their automation is described in `docs/agents/editing.md`. Not verified by ear.
- The phaser, the chorus and a sequence of waves can each follow the tempo (`phaser.sync`, `chorus.sync`, `sample.sync`, each a `RateSync` note value). `syncedRateHz` in the engine is the one place a note value becomes a rate; `rateHz` in `rateSync.ts` is what the effects call, live (`updateEffects`, which runs again when the tempo changes) and in export, and `toVoiceParams` does the same for a sequence's speed. Off is stored as nothing, so a control sends `'off'` and the sanitizer drops it; the store's merge would otherwise keep the old value. The owner asked on 2026-10-12 that everything with a rate has a sync: give a new rate control one. Every `RATE_SYNCS` menu has dotted (`1/4d`, `1/8d`) and triplet (`1/4t`, `1/8t`, `1/16t`) values as well, slowest first; a value's length in beats is `RATE_SYNC_BEATS` in `voiceParams.ts`.
- The shared delay can follow the tempo (`delay.sync`, a note value). `delaySeconds` in `delayTime.ts` is the one place that turns it into seconds, for live playback (`setEffectsTempo`) and export. `sync` is optional and absent when off, so older projects still match the expected shape.
- The chorus, the master EQ and the reverb's pre-delay and damping came after the first four effects. They are optional in `EffectsLoopState` and must stay out of the defaults passed to `localService`: `restore()` reports a project as damaged when its effects do not match the defaults' shape. `sanitizeEffects` adds them back by hand, and `effectSettings.ts` holds what the controls show until they are set. Only a new project (`blankState`) starts with a shaped reverb.
- With no pre-delay and no damping `reverbImpulse` is the original impulse, sample for sample, and a flat EQ is left out of an export altogether, so older projects render as they did.
- `createChorus` and `createMasterEq` in `browserAudio.ts` build those effects for live playback and for export. The EQ is on the whole mix and works with the effects loop off.
- The shared delay and reverb each have a Presets menu (`services/effectPresets.ts`: a slapback, a dub delay and a quarter echo; a small room and a long hall). A preset sets every setting of its effect and switches it on, through the box's own `onChange`, so it is one change; it spells out `sync: 'off'` and `kind: 'room'`, which the store's merge would otherwise keep from before. `effectPresets.test.ts` holds each to the limits the store keeps. The drive, phaser and chorus have Presets menus too, and among the presets are clones of well-known units, named "… style" (the owner's list of 2026-10-19; their final names are still the owner's): Space Echo and Memory Man on the delay, a Plate on the reverb, Juno I, Juno II and Dimension on the chorus, Small Stone and Phase 90 on the phaser, and Tube Screamer, RAT and Big Muff on the drive. They needed three settings, all optional and absent meaning as before: `delay.color` (the repeats pass through a low-pass, `delayColorHz`, so each is darker than the last), `delay.wow` (two slow oscillators on the delay's time, `createWow`) and `drive.kind` (`overdrive`, `distortion` or `fuzz`, each its own curve in `driveCurve`; Classic is stored as nothing). `normalizeEffectsLoop` in `useStudio` rebuilds the delay and the drive from a list of their fields, so all three are in that list, always sent (0 or `classic`) so that turning them off gets through the store's merge; a new field left out of it never reaches the store, which is what the browser test caught here. Live, the delay always has the colour filter and the wow, set to nothing; an export builds them only where they are set, so a project that never set them renders the graph it always did. The Spring is a reverb kind of its own (below), with a "Spring style" preset.
- The Hall's Decay knob works in Chrome as designed (roadmap 14, closed 2026-10-20). It once seemed not to, because `hall-reverb.spec.ts` measured reverb tails on top of the test's kick, which rings quietly on by itself for about three seconds: every take, Room or Hall, read about three seconds whatever the knob said. The spec now takes the kick dry and measures each reverb with it subtracted, sample by sample, and holds the knob both ways by its late level. A decay read off a reverb fed that kick cannot come out under about three seconds, so measure a tail's length with a short input. When a measurement in Chrome disagrees with `audioGraph.ts`, take a dry take first.
- The Spring (`reverb.kind` 'spring', roadmap 15, `createSpringReverb` in `browserAudio.ts`) is a guitar amplifier's spring tank: two tanks, one a side, each one loop of its delay, then a chain of all-passes (`SPRING_DISPERSION`, around `SPRING_HZ`), then a low-pass the damping lowers (`springDampHz`), then the feedback (`hallFeedback`); a high-pass and more all-passes on the way in. The all-passes hold frequencies near a kilohertz back longer than the highs, so each echo chirps more than the one before: the drip. It keeps the Hall's rules for loops, and `springReverb.test.ts` holds its decay to the knob, its level to the Hall's (`springLevel`), its result to either order of pulling inputs, the highs arriving first and damping darkening the tail. The live engine and export build it as they build the Hall (`createBuiltReverb`; `bus.hall` holds whichever of the two is sounding, with its `kind`). Stored as 'spring'; the Room is still stored as nothing. Not verified by ear: how much it chirps is a guess at a spring's sound.
- The reverb has two kinds (`reverb.kind`): Room, the convolver every project had, stored as nothing (the control sends `'room'` and the sanitizer drops it, as `sync` is dropped), and Hall, `createHallReverb` in `browserAudio.ts`: Freeverb's shape, six damped feedback combs a side into three all-passes, built from native nodes by the one function for live playback and export. Its decay, damping, pre-delay and level are settings of those nodes (`set`), so only choosing it builds one; `AudioEngine.replaceReverb` crossfades between kinds and cuts the old one loose. A project that never chose it exports the same graph as before; a test pins it.
- Loops of native nodes, as Chrome renders them (a probe on the check machines, pull request 188): a loop is one render quantum (128 frames) longer than its delay, so a comb of 1000 frames rings at 1000, 2128, 3256 (this is true of the shared delay's repeats too), and a loop with two ways out, or two loops that feed each other, renders differently from run to run because the order a node's inputs are pulled in changes. So in the Hall every loop has one way out (its delay), no loop feeds another, no node adds more than two signals, `HALL_QUANTUM` is counted into each loop's length, and every delay is a whole number of frames of its context (a delay between frames is interpolated, which would dull live playback against an export). `ui/test/helpers/audioGraph.ts` renders a graph the same way, and can pull inputs in either order; `hallReverb.test.ts` plays the Hall through it to measure its decay and level (`hallLevel` holds it within half a decibel of the Room at the same mix) and to show the order does not matter. Change a length, the diffusion or the damping and re-measure. Anything else built with a loop must keep to the same rules or its exports will not repeat.
- Two exports of the same project can differ in a few samples by one step of a 16-bit file: the browser's offline renderer rounds a kick through the reverb differently from run to run. Browser tests that compare exports allow for it (`same` in `effects.spec.ts`).
- Everything live playback keeps is a field of one `AudioEngine` (`browserAudio.ts`): the context, the master gain, EQ, limiter and safety shaper, the two effect buses, the worklet load, the volume, mute, effects and tempo it was last given, and what is sounding (samples, metronome clicks, lanes, a reverb or drive being faded out). `audioEngine` is the app's one, made when the module loads; its context is made on first use. The module's functions (`getAudioContext`, `createAudioLane`, `setEffectsLoop` and the rest) are one-line calls to it. The audio hooks (`useSynthAudio(engine)`, `useDrumAudio(engine)`), the guest audio (`createGuestPlayer`, `createGuestTap`) and the studio (`useStudio(engine)`, kept in the core as `engine`) are handed the engine, the app's one by default, and call it directly (roadmap 13); kids mode and the rest of the app still use the module's functions, and `midiTime` in `studioState.ts` still reads the app's engine. A test can hand any of them an engine of its own (`audio-engine.test.ts` tries the guest player that way). `dispose()` stops and disconnects all of it and closes the context; nothing in the app calls it, it is there for tests and for tearing down. An engine used after that makes a new context and keeps the settings it was given, as it does when the browser closes a context. The builders `wavExport.ts` uses (`createChorus`, `createMasterEq`, `createHallReverb`, `reverbImpulse`, `eqGains`, `chorusSweep`, `configureLimiter`, `scheduleDuck`, the curves) are plain functions and must not come to depend on the engine. A test makes an engine of its own by passing `new AudioEngine(...)` a function that returns a fake context (`audio-engine.test.ts`).
- Kick ducking is Web Audio gain automation, not core DSP: `scheduleDuck` in `browserAudio.ts` is called by the live scheduler and by `wavExport.ts`, so both pump identically. Export gets its kick times from `drumHits`, the same list the drum render uses.
- Each drum has a share of its own of the kit's delay send and reverb send (`DrumSettings.delaySend`, `reverbSend`, 0 to 1), set by the Delay and Reverb knobs under Sends in the drum strip. The owner's decision (2026-10-13): those two effects only, with the kit's own sends (`drumFx.sends`) as a master over them. What reaches the delay from a drum is the drum, times its share, times the kit's send. Absent means 1, all of it, which is what every drum sent before; `sanitizeDrums` stores a share only when it is under 1, so a project that never set one is unchanged in storage, in its hash for sync and in sound. The knob sends 1 and the checks drop it. A share cannot go above 1: a drum is kept out of an effect, never sent more than the kit.
- `DrumCore.process` takes two more places to write, and fills them with a second and a third mix of the same voices, each scaled by its drum's share where it is added to the kit's sum, so pan, level, choke, an imported sample and a recorded cymbal are alike in all three. With every share at 1 they are the kit sample for sample, and the kit is bit-identical whether or not they are asked for; `drumSends.test.ts` checks both. A share is read when a hit starts, as its volume and pan are. Live, the drum processor has three outputs and the drums' lane is made with `createAudioLane('drums', ['delay', 'reverb'])`: `taps` are inputs with the lane's volume and ducking whose only way on is the kit's send to that effect, and the kit's signal no longer feeds those two sends. In an export `renderDrumMixes` makes a mix only when a drum that plays has a share under 1, and `connectEffects` asks for it only when that effect is on and sent to; otherwise the graph is the one it always was. Drive, phaser and chorus are the whole kit's. A stem of the drums has its effects.
- A kit change with its defaults lays volume, tone, the second knob, tune, humanize and pan over each drum and leaves the shares, as it leaves the cymbal type; `drum.reset` clears them. Kids mode sends no shares, so its drums are what they were.
